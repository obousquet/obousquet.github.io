# Operation-Driven Calculus Construction

Use when mathematical actions suggest a language of constructions or arguments,
including when the operations and their algebra are still being discovered. A
single revealing phenomenon can suffice. A table of operations is a starting
point; the output is a proved part of the calculus and its concrete interpretation.

## Construct And Test The Candidate

1. Recover representative arguments, examples, and corrections. Extract actions
   rather than common prose or tactic sequences. A single revealing operation
   can suffice; a large proof corpus is optional.
2. For each operation record its input/output types, domain, side conditions,
   retained data, and intended quantitative conclusion. Include source dependence,
   signs, regularity, constants, or scale information when the proof uses them.
3. Generate small well-typed composites. Compare their concrete meanings and test
   proposed identities, inequalities, units, order effects, and error accumulation.
   Preserve witnesses to failed laws. Enumeration is mechanical only for the
   effectively specified and bounded part of the language.
4. Refine the representation, rule, or object class according to the failure.
   Do not cure an invalid independence step by simply adding its conclusion as
   an axiom. A valid identity with an uncontrolled norm needs an estimate.
5. Construct concrete models and prove primitive validity and compatibility.
   Separate algebraic relations from existence, regularity, and quantitative
   bounds. Specify whether relations preserve interpretations, annotations, or
   both; identical interpretations can admit bounds of different sharpness.
6. Prove the appropriate generating theorem, then try a composite or model not
   used to design the rules. Recovering the design examples checks consistency;
   additional applications test scope without establishing completeness.

Steps can interact. A central reconstruction, extension, or stability theorem may
emerge only after considerable development of the models.

## A Precise Finite-Expression Soundness Pattern

Choose sorts s, typed operations o:(s1,...,sk)->s, concrete sets V_s, annotation
sets A_s, and a property P_s(x,c). An annotation may contain an estimate, domain,
provenance, or error. Give each operation a possibly partial concrete
interpretation val_o and an annotation update Phi_o. Prove, on the stated domain:

    all P_sj(xj,cj) and Adm_o(x1,...,xk;c1,...,ck)
      imply val_o(x1,...,xk) is defined and
             P_s(val_o(x1,...,xk), Phi_o(c1,...,ck)).

Every finite expression with certified leaves and proved admissibility at every
node then has a defined interpretation and its propagated certificate. Prove this
by structural induction. The substantive work is in the primitive/model theorems;
a leaf or admissibility assumption encoding the desired conclusion leaves the
original obligation open. A readout theorem must translate the root certificate
to the intended mathematical result.

If syntax is quotiented by relations, prove the interpretation descends and the
claimed annotation behavior is compatible. Infinite expressions, renormalized
limits, or completed spaces require their own convergence and continuity results.
Soundness alone establishes neither expressive completeness nor optimal bounds.

## Let The Operations Suggest The Algebra

| Observed behavior | Candidate to investigate | Proof still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Addition, scaling, cancellation | Vector or graded space with bilinear control | Linearity of interpretation and the actual estimate |
| Repeated coordinate or scale changes | Group or semigroup action | Composition and transformation of model bounds |
| Nested substitutions | Typed trees and substitution operations | Units, domains, associativity, overlap compatibility |
| Order of operations matters | Operators and commutators | Exact relations and independent analytic control |
| Local descriptions must agree | Transition data and descent | Compatibility and existence of assembled models |
| Elimination leaves the class | Latent presentations or enlarged factors | Closure and comparison with original quantities |
| Cutoff-dependent values diverge | Counterterms and a completion | Model preservation, uniform estimates, convergence |

These are candidate investigations, not deductions that a Hopf algebra or a
cohomology theory exists. A commutator identity gives no norm bound by itself.

## Example: Repair Addition By Tracking Signed Sources

Let independent centered X_j have certificates
E exp(lambda X_j) <= exp(v_j lambda^2/2) for every real lambda, v_j >= 0.
For expressions generated by 0, source symbols, real scaling, and addition,
retain the coefficient vector c, interpreted as sum_j c_j X_j. Coefficients add
and scale exactly. Independence of the original sources proves the certificate
with V(c)=sum_j v_j c_j^2 for every expression, even if a source is reused.

Consequently V(c+d)=V(c)+V(d)+2 sum_j v_j c_j d_j. A single source with v=1
has V(X+X)=4 and V(X-X)=0. Retaining only marginal certificates and adding their
parameters loses both repeated-source dependence and cancellation. Keeping source
labels alone can enforce disjointness; retaining signed coefficients supports the
larger linear language. Products and suprema still require different model rules.

This is classical linear concentration, used to show a complete construction:
failed rule, refined data, closed operations, model theorem, and tail readout.
The algebra organizes the proof; source independence and the supplied exponential
bounds carry its probabilistic content.
